Frequently asked questions

How do I install an English–Persian dictionary on a Kindle?

Connect the Kindle by USB, copy English-Persian.Dictionary.v3.1-classic.mobi into the documents/dictionaries folder, and eject. In a book, long-press a word, tap the dictionary name in the pop-up and choose English-Persian Dictionary v3.1, or set it under Settings > Language & Dictionaries > Dictionaries > English.

How do I add this dictionary to a Kobo?

Copy dicthtml-en-fa.zip into the .kobo/custom-dict folder on the Kobo (create it if missing), eject, then select it under Settings > Language and dictionaries. Firmware 4.24 or later is needed.

Does it work with KOReader, Onyx Boox and PocketBook?

Yes, through the StarDict build. Unzip English-Persian-v3.1-stardict.zip into koreader/data/dict/ for KOReader (which runs on Kindle, Kobo, PocketBook, Boox, reMarkable and Android), or into the dictionaries folder of GoldenDict, Moon+ Reader and other StarDict apps. PocketBook's built-in dictionary uses its own format, which is not provided; use KOReader there.

What is the difference between the classic and right-to-left Kindle builds?

Kindle dictionary pop-ups on E Ink devices lay text out left to right, so the classic build, like v2.4, stores each Persian definition with its words in reverse order so that it displays correctly. The right-to-left build stores natural reading order with right-to-left paragraphs for Kindle apps and firmware that handle bidirectional text. Try classic first; use the right-to-left build if sentences look reversed.

Is the English–Persian dictionary free?

Yes. It is released under the MIT licence, the same as the upstream project it is based on, and works offline.

What is new in v3.1 compared with v2.4?

164,524 entries instead of 145,075, including 21,578 new entries. v3.0 added modern vocabulary (internet, smartphone, podcast), science, medicine, business, phrasal verbs, idioms and slang; v3.1 added twelve subject sections (philosophy and logic, earth and environmental sciences, statistics, arts, literature, history and archaeology, sociology and anthropology, education, agriculture and veterinary science, transport and logistics, media, fashion and textiles), much more slang, and 3,589 subject senses on existing words. The 7,016 most frequent words were rewritten with the most common sense first. Persian spelling was normalised in 106,983 entries, 3,120 misspelled headwords were corrected, 2,128 duplicates were merged, and 31,189 inflected forms were indexed so that looking up 'walked' opens 'walk'.

Were the new translations reviewed by a human editor?

No. The rewritten and new entries were written by AI language models following written lexicographic guidelines and have not been reviewed by a human Persian editor. About 107,000 specialised entries received only mechanical repairs and keep their original wording. Corrections are welcome as GitHub issues.

Know before you rely on it

  • New and rewritten wording was produced by AI language models and has not been reviewed by a human Persian editor.
  • About 107,000 specialised entries received mechanical repairs only and keep their 1990s wording.
  • Built and checked by software, not screen by screen on every e-reader. The right-to-left Kindle build has not been tried on a device.
